# ARO — Growth and Network Density

## Growth thesis

ARO grows when sharing and participation create a real opportunity—not when attention loops maximize screen time. Density, successful outcomes, and participant-to-host conversion come before geographic breadth.

## Calgary first

Calgary is the first density market. The working narrative is: **“Calgary is becoming the first ARO City.”** ARO should prove repeatable local liquidity before expanding city by city.

## Founding supply

**ARO Founding 100** is an intentional recruitment program for the first high-potential hosts, beginning with the language vertical and safe adjacent formats only after category approval. Founders receive onboarding, format coaching, ARO Director support, direct feedback access, and recognition—not unqualified earnings promises.

Primary acquisition promise: **PEOPLE NEAR YOU WANT WHAT YOU KNOW.**

Exploratory campaign: **WHAT ARE YOU WORTH OUTSIDE YOUR JOB?** Use carefully: it should reveal human capability, not reduce personal worth to money.

## Compounding loops

### Consumer to Creator

A participant attends, records a meaningful outcome, discovers a capability they could share, and is invited into an appropriate host pathway. Trust/category requirements still apply.

### Opportunity Unlocks

Example: four of six participants are committed; two more unlock the Circle. Sharing has direct utility because it helps the opportunity become viable. UI must state the threshold, commitment type, deadline, and cancellation terms without coercion.

### Wish Markets

Demand may accumulate before supply exists. Show aggregate, privacy-preserving intent and invite qualified hosts only after minimum signal and category controls are met.

### Empty Table Project

With consent, show a partner business’s unused capacity before ARO and meaningful use afterward: filled quiet hours, community value, and incremental revenue. Do not fabricate counterfactuals.

### Real Life Challenge

Invite people to replace discretionary screen time with learning, earning, meaningful connection, or exploration. Measure completed real-life activity; avoid guilt, punitive streaks, or health claims.

## Creators and brands

Creator Guild Leaders and creator-certified experience formats are future acquisition mechanisms. Certification must mean a defined format/quality standard, not substitute for ARO category qualification.

Brands may fund access, equipment, spaces, or useful opportunity formats. Sponsorship is disclosed and may not purchase Trust status, recommendation priority without labeling, user data, or safety exceptions.

Hypothetical examples include a camera brand funding beginner photo walks or a café group funding quiet-hour language Circles. These are examples, not announced partnerships.

## Metrics

| Dimension | Metrics |
|---|---|
| Supply | host application → approval, host activation, time to first published opportunity, time to first booking |
| Liquidity | city/category density, Circle fill rate, time to threshold, viable demand per active host |
| Loops | participant → host conversion, invite conversion, Opportunity Unlock share and conversion rate |
| Outcomes | attendance, completion, repeat participation, real-life hours, verified host earnings |
| Retention | participant and host cohort retention driven by completed value, not passive app opens |
| Acquisition | organic share, creator/host-sourced activation, partner-sourced activation, blended CAC |

Definitions and instrumentation must be locked before public claims. Growth experiments may not weaken consent, Trust gates, pricing clarity, or user control.

## Expansion gate

Do not launch the next city or category until the current market demonstrates adequate host activation, fill rate, attendance, support capacity, safety performance, and repeat behavior. Expansion thresholds are founder decisions informed by measured Calgary cohorts.
